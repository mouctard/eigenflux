Stellarators confine plasma with a fully three-dimensional, non-axisymmetric magnetic field — there's no 2D poloidal cross-section to reduce the problem to, unlike the tokamak page. Solving a 3D MHD equilibrium (with a real solver like DESC or VMEC) is genuinely heavy — not something to run fresh in a browser tab.

Where this data comes from

These flux surfaces are not solved live. They're read from precomputed equilibria published in the DESC repository itself — real DESC-solved solutions for real stellarator configurations, exported offline (tools/export_stellarators.py) to a compact binary format this page just reads and renders. Four nested flux surfaces per configuration (at normalized radius ρ = 0.3, 0.55, 0.8, 1.0) are shown, colored by nesting depth with the same colormap as the tokamak page.

The configurations

  • Wendelstein 7-X — the operating superconducting stellarator at Max Planck IPP in Greifswald, Germany. 5 field periods, optimized for quasi-isodynamic confinement.
  • HSX — the Helically Symmetric Experiment at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. 4 field periods, the first stellarator built around quasi-helical symmetry.
  • NCSX — the National Compact Stellarator Experiment design from PPPL. 3 field periods, quasi-axisymmetric.

Units and caveats

Coordinates are in meters, at each device's actual real-world scale (W7-X's major radius is several times HSX's, and the two look it here). This page shows geometry — flux-surface shape — not the pressure/current profiles or stability properties those equilibria also contain; a future pass could add those as an overlay.

Live fuel burn, driven by a real plasma volume

Unlike the tokamak page (whose fixed-boundary solve gives no free-boundary plasma volume of its own), this page's flux surfaces already are a real solved 3D boundary, so a real enclosed volume can be computed directly: for each configuration's outermost surface, V = (1/6)|Σ v₀·(v₁×v₂)| summed over the same triangles the viewer already renders — the divergence theorem applied to a closed mesh, the general 3D analog of the tokamak page's Pappus's-theorem volume. Checked against a synthetic torus of exactly known volume before use (tools/validate_stellarator_volume.mjs, converges cleanly, sub-1% error at the resolution actually shipped), and independently against a real published figure: this computation gives 27.9 m³ for W7-X's boundary, against 30 m³ published for the real device — a solid match for an independent geometric calculation from a different equilibrium variant.

From there it's the exact same 0D burn model as the tokamak page (same fuel presets, same energy-capture presets, same closed-form deplete/sustained math) — see its "Choosing a fuel" and "Where the energy goes" panels for the physics. Temperature and density are still illustrative reactor-ballpark numbers (matching the tokamak page's own operating points), not these experiments' real (far colder and thinner) operating conditions — W7-X, HSX, and NCSX are research devices studying confinement physics, not power reactors, so pairing their real geometry with reactor-scale conditions is a what-if extrapolation, the same one the tokamak page already makes.

Uses a vendored, MIT-licensed copy of three.js for rendering — the only dependency in this project. Nothing else is fetched from anywhere at runtime.
