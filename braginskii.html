ℹ How this works

This page time-steps the real Braginskii two-fluid equations (density continuity, separate ion/electron energy equations) on a 2D poloidal cross-section, using a wall boundary built from real, digitized ITER divertor/inboard/top-of-machine geometry (from SOLPS-ITER's own DivGeo tutorial data) plus an illustrative outboard closing arc — see tools/export_iter_equilibrium.py and src/braginskii/wallGeometry.js. The background magnetic field (used to split transport into fast parallel / slow perpendicular components) is the same real ITER equilibrium used on the tokamak page's "Show real ITER shape" overlay, extended into the SOL since that equilibrium's own grid genuinely reaches past the last closed flux surface.

Direct reference for this page's model and boundary conditions: W. Dekeyser, P. Boerner, S. Voskoboynikov, V.A. Rozhansky, I. Senichenkov, L. Kaveeva, I. Veselova, E. Vekshina, X. Bonnin, R.A. Pitts, M. Baelmans, "Plasma edge simulations including realistic wall geometry with SOLPS-ITER," Nuclear Materials and Energy 27 (2021) 100999, doi:10.1016/j.nme.2021.100999 (open access). Parallel closures (κ∥, viscosity, electron-ion energy equilibration) are the real formulas from the NRL Plasma Formulary (2019 revision), itself citing S.I. Braginskii, "Transport Processes in a Plasma," Reviews of Plasma Physics Vol. 1 (1965) — verified against the formulary's own text before being used here (see src/braginskii/closures.js), not transcribed from memory. Sheath boundary conditions use that paper's generalized Bohm-Chodura form (its eq. 7/9/10/11, no-drift limit), including its incidence-angle projection — needed here because the wall is a real shape, so field-incidence angle genuinely varies node to node, unlike a flux-surface boundary where it's ~constant by construction.
